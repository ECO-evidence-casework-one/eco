# ECO publisher and stewardship gate

**Authority:** controlling only when present on canonical `main`  
**Issue:** #17  
**Current decision:** no official ECO publisher or steward is appointed  
**Release effect:** blocks official ordinary-user and institutional distribution until accepted and evidenced

## 1. Plain-English position

ECO may continue as a public free and open-source **source-development project** using synthetic and non-sensitive material.

ECO must not make or endorse an official ordinary-user binary release, institutional supply arrangement or supported deployment until an established legal organisation has knowingly accepted the publisher and stewardship duties in this record.

This record does not:

- appoint an organisation or individual;
- create a company, charity, community interest company or other body;
- make the originating developer or another contributor a director, trustee, publisher, supplier, controller, support operator or liability owner;
- approve real evidence, signing, a binary, release or deployment;
- restrict lawful rights under ECO's open-source licence.

## 2. Preferred organisational route

The preferred route is an **existing established organisation** with a compatible public-interest and open-source mission, such as:

- an established nonprofit or charity;
- a public-interest technology organisation;
- an open-source foundation or community steward;
- an appropriate university, public-interest institution or equivalent body;
- another established legal organisation that can prove it will preserve ECO's offline, free and open-source and user-data non-custody boundaries.

The project does **not** presently require the originating developer to form a new company, CIC, charity or other entity.

A proposal to create a new entity is a separate future decision. It must identify who would become directors, trustees, members or equivalent office-holders, what legal and reporting duties they would accept, how the entity would be funded and operated, and why an established host cannot perform the role. No contributor may be placed into such an office by default or implication.

## 3. Contribution is not appointment

Writing code, documentation or tests; opening issues; owning a GitHub account; administering a repository; or originating the project does not by itself constitute project appointment as:

- legal publisher, manufacturer, provider, supplier or distributor;
- contracting party;
- company director, charity trustee or equivalent office-holder;
- controller or processor for users' local case material;
- security-response, support, privacy, accessibility or complaints service;
- signing-certificate owner or sole release authority;
- insurer, indemnifier or personal liability bearer;
- professional legal, clinical, forensic or regulatory adviser.

This governance rule does not decide whether duties can arise from **actual conduct or applicable law**. A person or organisation may acquire responsibilities by officially publishing, signing, supplying, contracting, controlling data flows, making product claims or otherwise acting in a legally significant role.

Until an accountable organisation, authority and role allocation are documented, no individual contributor may act on behalf of ECO to:

- publish or sign an official ordinary-user release;
- contract for institutional supply;
- accept warranties, indemnities or service obligations;
- receive real user evidence or support diagnostics;
- make legal, medical, forensic, compliance or regulatory claims;
- present themselves as ECO's official publisher, supplier or professional service.

## 4. ECO boundaries a steward must preserve

An acceptable steward must commit in writing to preserve:

- free and open-source project code and compliant bundled components;
- fully offline application operation;
- no required account, cloud service, telemetry, advertising or analytics;
- no routine developer or publisher access to users' evidence;
- one self-contained Windows application as the intended official delivery model;
- original evidence separated from derived readings and generated suggestions;
- source-linked and user-controlled evidence assistance;
- the medical, legal-services, forensic, profiling, scoring and official-decision boundaries in the intended-purpose control;
- meaningful accessibility and low-spec Windows support as product requirements;
- truthful claims, provenance, licensing, signing and release records.

A proposed steward fails the gate if it requires proprietary relicensing, a closed official fork, cloud evidence upload, user accounts, routine telemetry, hidden online services, routine access to users' case files or personal assignment of organisational duties to an individual contributor.

## 5. Minimum organisational capability

Before acceptance, the proposed steward must prove that it:

- has an established legal identity and governing authority;
- can enter contracts in its own name;
- has authorised decision-makers and deputies;
- can maintain financial, governance and audit records;
- can operate monitored private and public communication routes;
- can fund or obtain legal, privacy, security, accessibility and insurance advice where needed;
- can maintain releases, warnings and continuity when an individual maintainer is unavailable;
- can preserve the open-source and offline product boundaries;
- can decline or stop a release when evidence is incomplete.

The organisation's legal name, status, jurisdiction, governing body, authorised signatories, service address or formal notice route and principal operational contacts must be recorded.

## 6. Duties that must be accepted

### 6.1 Official release authority

The steward must:

- decide whether a candidate may become an official ECO release;
- require all active release gates to be satisfied;
- reconcile the exact source, executable, manifest, SBOM, licences, notices, hashes and signature;
- maintain the authoritative official release route and verification instructions;
- ensure no official file is changed after signing;
- preserve approval, refusal, withdrawal and end-of-support decisions;
- issue warnings, revoke approval or withdraw an official release when material risk is discovered.

Official release authority applies only to project-endorsed ECO releases. It does not control independent GPL forks or redistributions.

### 6.2 Security and vulnerability response

The steward must:

- operate and monitor a private vulnerability-reporting route;
- publish supported versions and realistic response expectations;
- triage and preserve confidential reports;
- coordinate fixes, mitigations, advisories and withdrawal;
- communicate significant incidents promptly and clearly;
- provide updates until resolution and preserve a post-incident record;
- maintain a vulnerability policy suitable for free and open-source development.

Public repository issues must not be the only route for sensitive vulnerability reports.

### 6.3 Maintenance, updates and end of support

The steward must:

- define the support period for each official release;
- identify which releases receive security fixes;
- publish safe offline update and rollback instructions;
- plan for vulnerable or unsupported bundled models, runtimes and parsers;
- publish end-of-support information through stable channels;
- preserve a user-controlled export and exit route.

Stable public notices are the baseline for anonymous distribution. Targeted notice must also be used where applicable law, contract, an institutional deployment or legitimately held contact information requires or reasonably enables it.

ECO must not add accounts, telemetry or mandatory registration merely to build a contact list without a separate approved privacy and product assessment.

### 6.4 Privacy, support data and complaints

For every distribution and support model, the steward must document:

- the actual data flows;
- whether it is controller, joint controller, processor or neither for each activity;
- whether support, diagnostics or complaints may contain personal data;
- lawful purpose, minimisation, access, retention, deletion and breach arrangements where applicable;
- how users can obtain support without publishing real evidence;
- how privacy, accessibility, security and product complaints are separated and escalated.

Where the organisation handles personal data, it must operate the complaint process required by current data-protection law, including a clear complaint route, acknowledgement within the applicable period, appropriate investigation and communication of the outcome.

### 6.5 Accessibility and harmful-output response

The steward must provide monitored routes for:

- accessibility barriers and reasonable adjustments;
- materially misleading or harmful generated output;
- evidence-provenance and source-linking failures;
- data loss, corruption or unintended disclosure;
- legal, medical, forensic, profiling, scoring or high-consequence boundary failures.

Accessibility approval must rely on objective evidence, specialist testing and representative disabled-user testing where appropriate—not automated scanning alone.

The steward must not imply that ECO is an emergency or safeguarding service or that help has been contacted when it has not.

### 6.6 Signing, repository and continuity control

The steward must:

- govern signing requests, certificates, keys, revocation and recovery;
- separate authorship, review and release approval where practical;
- protect release branches and require reviewed pull requests for ordinary changes;
- restrict and log emergency administrative bypass;
- maintain more than one protected recovery owner where the platform permits;
- preserve recovery instructions outside one person's device or account;
- test repository, organisation and signing recovery;
- define who may freeze, archive, transfer or restore official project resources.

No single unrecoverable personal account, device or certificate may be the sole long-term continuity mechanism.

### 6.7 Official name, identity and endorsement

Before an official release, the steward must publish an official-project identity policy covering:

- the authoritative repository and release route;
- the accountable organisation;
- exact source commit, manifest, hashes and signature verification;
- use of the ECO name, logo and official-release description;
- how independent forks and redistributions must avoid false endorsement or consumer confusion;
- how the project will warn about misleading builds without implying responsibility for independent third parties.

The open-source licence permits covered copying, modification and conveyance. It does not by itself authorise a party to describe an independent build as an official ECO release or imply project endorsement.

### 6.8 Contracts, procurement, liability and insurance

Before institutional supply, the steward must decide and record:

- the contracting legal entity and authorised signatory;
- the supplied product and support scope;
- intended purpose and prohibited uses;
- licence, maintenance and end-of-support terms;
- data-role allocation;
- security, accessibility, incident and complaint responsibilities;
- liability allocation, warranties and insurance position;
- termination, exit, portability and withdrawal arrangements;
- accurate AI and supply-chain disclosures requested by the buyer.

Buyer-side policies such as PPN 017 may create questions or contractual requirements for in-scope procurements; they are not treated here as universal supplier law.

An individual contributor must not make warranties, contracts or indemnities on behalf of a non-existent or unappointed organisation.

### 6.9 Regulatory and jurisdiction decisions

Before the project approves availability outside the currently assessed source-development context, the steward must own and preserve the necessary feature-, role-, market- and deployment-specific assessment.

This includes, where relevant:

- UK medical-device and other intended-purpose boundaries;
- public-sector and high-consequence decision use;
- justice-sector or forensic use;
- EU AI Act provider, deployer, importer, distributor or other roles;
- EU Cyber Resilience Act manufacturer, open-source software steward or other roles;
- product safety, product liability and consumer obligations;
- mandatory reporting, conformity, representative or market-surveillance arrangements.

This document does not determine those classifications in advance.

## 7. Steward selection and acceptance process

### Stage 1 — public-safe exploration

Permitted now:

- prepare a public-safe project and partner information pack;
- discuss ECO using public repository information and synthetic examples;
- ask organisations about mission fit, open-source capability and operational capacity;
- record non-binding interest.

Not permitted:

- announcing an official publisher;
- transferring repository, signing or legal duties;
- real-data pilots;
- institutional commitments;
- implying endorsement, regulatory approval or deployment readiness.

### Stage 2 — due diligence

The candidate organisation must complete the separate publisher acceptance checklist and provide evidence for its legal identity, governance, open-source commitment, security response, complaints, accessibility, signing, continuity, funding and support capacity.

### Stage 3 — governing-body acceptance

The organisation's authorised governing body must approve a written acceptance record that states:

- exact duties accepted;
- duties not accepted;
- authorised role-holders and deputies;
- funding and operational resources;
- conditions and dependencies;
- effective date, review date and withdrawal process.

A staff member's informal support or exploratory email is not organisational acceptance.

### Stage 4 — operational validation

Before release, synthetic tests or tabletop exercises must prove the principal routes and controls, including vulnerability reporting, complaint handling, release withdrawal, incident communication, repository recovery and maintainer unavailability.

### Stage 5 — separate release decision

Passing this organisational gate does not itself approve a release. The exact release candidate must still satisfy all application, evidence-integrity, security, accessibility, provenance, licensing, signing and distribution gates.

## 8. Minimum acceptance evidence

The gate cannot pass without:

- legal identity and governing-authority evidence;
- formal acceptance decision;
- named authorised role-holders and deputies;
- role and permission register;
- supported-version and end-of-support policy;
- private vulnerability disclosure and incident-response process;
- release, update, rollback, withdrawal and signing procedures;
- privacy and deployment data-role matrix;
- privacy, accessibility, security, product-complaint and support routes;
- official-project identity and endorsement policy;
- continuity and recovery plan;
- institutional contracting, liability and insurance decision record where relevant;
- completed synthetic or tabletop tests;
- a current review against the intended-purpose, release and reporting controls.

## 9. Stop rules

Official ordinary-user release remains blocked where:

- no accountable organisation has formally accepted the role;
- duties depend on an individual contributor's informal or spare-time capacity;
- security, complaints, maintenance, signing, withdrawal or continuity routes are not operational;
- official identity and verification are undefined;
- actual-build provenance and licensing are incomplete;
- any P0 or release-relevant P1 remains unresolved.

Official institutional supply remains blocked where:

- no contracting legal entity and authorised signatory exist;
- support, data roles, liability, insurance, exit or incident arrangements are unresolved;
- the organisation cannot provide accurate buyer-requested disclosures;
- the proposed use exceeds the intended-purpose boundary;
- any P0 or release-relevant P1 remains unresolved.

Healthcare, justice-sector, authority-side high-consequence or EU availability remains blocked until the responsible organisation completes and accepts the relevant legal, regulatory and deployment assessment.

## 10. Acceptance criteria for this governance record

Before issue #17 can close:

- this record is merged into canonical `main` after exact-head review;
- the separate acceptance checklist is merged and usable;
- current public governance and role records do not contradict it;
- a named established organisation completes due diligence;
- its governing body formally accepts the required role;
- operational routes and continuity controls pass synthetic or tabletop testing;
- exact release and deployment responsibilities are documented;
- no individual contributor is assigned organisational duties merely through contribution;
- all remaining issue #17 acceptance tests are independently evidenced.

The governance document may be merged while issue #17 remains open. Issue #17 closes only after an organisation is actually accepted and the operational evidence exists.

This control has been checked against current official material including:

These sources do not appoint an organisation or predetermine every legal role. Duties depend on the actual conduct, product, claims, distribution model, data flows, market and deployment.
