# ECO named-organisation research method

**Issue:** #62  
**Purpose:** public-source research into possible established ECO steward organisations  
**Authority:** research control only when present on canonical `main`  
**Outreach effect:** none; this method does not approve contact, appointment or partnership

## 1. Controlling position

ECO has no appointed publisher, steward, partner or operating organisation.

The preferred future route is an existing established nonprofit, public-interest institution, open-source foundation, university or equivalent capable organisation that can preserve ECO's controlling boundaries.

This method permits structured public-source research only. It does not:

- imply that a named organisation is interested;
- recommend or appoint an organisation;
- authorise a message, meeting or application;
- transfer repository, signing, support or legal duties;
- create a company, CIC, charity or other entity;
- approve a real-data pilot, executable, release or deployment;
- assign organisational duties or personal liability to an individual contributor.

## 2. Research principles

### 2.1 Evidence before reputation

An organisation's public profile, size, mission language or previous partnerships are not proof that it can or will accept ECO's duties.

Every material finding must identify:

- the source;
- source owner or publisher;
- publication or reporting date where available;
- date accessed;
- the exact proposition supported;
- whether the finding is fact, inference or unknown;
- any limitation or conflict.

### 2.2 No positive assumption from silence

Missing evidence must not be converted into a favourable score.

Use:

- **PASS** — adequate current evidence supports the requirement;
- **HOLD** — material evidence is missing, ambiguous, outdated or conflicting;
- **FAIL** — reliable evidence shows incompatibility or inability;
- **NOT APPLICABLE** — a reasoned scope decision exists;
- **NOT ASSESSED** — research has not been performed.

### 2.3 Research is not outreach

Public-source research, non-binding discussion, due diligence and organisational acceptance are separate stages.

A provisional research status does not imply:

- interest;
- consent;
- endorsement;
- appointment;
- legal capability for every ECO role;
- release or deployment approval.

### 2.4 Current evidence controls

Use the newest reliable evidence for current organisational facts. Historical evidence may show experience but must not be treated as proof of current capability.

Record where:

- a programme has ended;
- governance or legal form changed;
- a policy is undated;
- a report relates to an older financial year;
- a named service or capability may no longer operate;
- current authority or funding cannot be confirmed.

## 3. Source hierarchy

### Tier A — primary authoritative evidence

Prefer:

- official legal or charity registers;
- filed accounts and annual reports;
- governing documents;
- board or trustee records;
- official policies and procedures;
- official programme, repository or service documentation;
- official security, privacy, accessibility and complaints pages;
- official procurement, grant or funding records;
- official source-code repositories and licence files.

### Tier B — first-party operational evidence

May include:

- official website pages;
- official blog posts and announcements;
- published case studies;
- staff or programme descriptions;
- official partner or funder listings;
- public job descriptions showing current operational capability.

Tier B evidence must not override conflicting Tier A evidence.

### Tier C — reliable independent evidence

May include:

- regulator or government publications;
- recognised funder records;
- university or institutional partner records;
- reputable journalism;
- independent technical or programme evaluations.

Use Tier C to corroborate or identify concerns, not to invent organisational authority.

### Tier D — weak or exploratory material

Examples:

- directory entries;
- conference biographies;
- social media;
- community discussion;
- unsourced summaries;
- search-result snippets.

Tier D may generate a research question but cannot support a PASS for a material requirement.

## 4. Research stages

### Stage 0 — category and scope definition

Record why the organisation entered the longlist and which possible role is being examined, for example:

- full publisher/steward;
- open-source or fiscal host;
- technical-maintenance collaborator;
- accessibility-review partner;
- public-interest research collaborator;
- legal, privacy or security support organisation.

Do not assume that capability for one role proves capability for another.

### Stage 1 — identity and existence screen

Confirm:

- legal name;
- legal form;
- registration number where applicable;
- jurisdiction;
- active status;
- principal public website;
- governing body or authority;
- current public contact route suitable for organisational enquiries.

A missing or unverified legal identity is a HOLD for publisher/steward consideration.

### Stage 2 — fatal exclusion screen

Apply the exclusions in section 6 before detailed scoring.

A confirmed fatal conflict produces **UNSUITABLE** for official ECO stewardship unless reliable evidence shows that the relevant position has changed.

### Stage 3 — capability evidence review

Assess each domain in section 7 independently.

A strong result in one domain must not compensate for a FAIL in a mandatory domain.

### Stage 4 — conflict and dependency review

Identify:

- commercial dependencies;
- funder or government dependencies;
- proprietary-platform commitments;
- cloud-first or data-access business models;
- political or institutional relationships that could affect user trust or independence;
- conflicts between the organisation's normal operating model and ECO's offline/no-custody boundary;
- reliance on one individual or time-limited grant;
- restrictions on licence, governance, name or project control.

The existence of funding or government work is not automatically disqualifying. Record the actual conflict or control risk.

### Stage 5 — provisional research decision

Use only:

- **POTENTIAL FIT** — no fatal exclusion found and adequate evidence supports all threshold domains for further due diligence;
- **HOLD** — potentially relevant, but material evidence is absent, stale, ambiguous or conflicting;
- **UNSUITABLE** — a fatal exclusion or material incompatibility is supported by reliable evidence;
- **NOT ASSESSED** — insufficient research performed.

`POTENTIAL FIT` means suitable for deeper due diligence only. It is not an outreach approval or recommendation of appointment.

## 5. Mandatory ECO boundaries

Every candidate for official stewardship must be compatible in principle with:

- free and open-source project code and compliant bundled components;
- fully offline normal application operation;
- no required ECO account;
- no required cloud service;
- no routine telemetry, analytics or advertising;
- no routine project or steward access to users' evidence;
- one self-contained Windows application as the intended delivery model;
- low-spec Windows support as a product objective;
- source-linked, user-controlled evidence assistance;
- visible separation of source, OCR, generated suggestions, user confirmation and user notes;
- the prohibited medical, legal-services, forensic, profiling, scoring and official-decision boundaries;
- truthful claims, actual-build provenance, licensing, signing and release records;
- support routes that do not require public submission of real evidence;
- no automatic assignment of organisational duties to an individual contributor.

These requirements are not balanced away by a high overall score.

## 6. Fatal exclusion screen

Record **UNSUITABLE** for official ECO stewardship where reliable current evidence shows that the organisation requires or would impose:

1. proprietary relicensing of ECO or withholding required corresponding source;
2. a closed official fork controlled outside ECO's open-source requirements;
3. mandatory cloud upload or remote processing of users' evidence;
4. mandatory accounts, routine telemetry, behavioural analytics or advertising;
5. routine organisational access to users' local case files;
6. real-user or real-data pilots before active evidence and release gates close;
7. unsupported claims that ECO is certified, compliant, secure, accurate, accessible, forensic, court-ready, medically safe or release-ready;
8. diagnosis, treatment, professional representation, forensic guarantees, person scoring or official adverse-decision use;
9. personal warranties, contracts, indemnities, support duties or liability assigned to an individual contributor by default;
10. dependence on one unfunded individual, personal account, device or signing credential for official continuity;
11. inability or refusal to operate vulnerability, complaint, release-withdrawal, signing and continuity duties;
12. inability to contract or act through an established legal identity where the proposed role requires it;
13. restrictions that prevent truthful public disclosure of governance, provenance, known limitations or adverse findings;
14. control arrangements materially incompatible with user-controlled evidence assistance or ECO's intended purpose.

Where evidence indicates a possible but unconfirmed conflict, record HOLD rather than FAIL.

## 7. Assessment domains

### Domain A — mission and public-interest fit

Assess whether current authoritative material supports alignment with:

- individual access to information, evidence or justice;
- digital rights, privacy or user agency;
- disability inclusion and accessible technology;
- public-interest or civic technology;
- sustainable open-source software;
- support for people dealing with public services or complex casework.

Mission fit alone cannot produce POTENTIAL FIT.

### Domain B — legal identity and governing authority

Assess:

- active legal status;
- governing body and decision authority;
- ability to enter contracts in its own name;
- filed accounts or equivalent financial records;
- conflicts-of-interest and governance arrangements;
- formal notice or service route;
- evidence that the proposed role falls within its powers or purpose.

### Domain C — FOSS and software stewardship

Assess evidence of:

- maintaining or hosting free/open-source software;
- public repositories and licence compliance;
- contributor governance;
- release management;
- dependency and supply-chain management;
- long-term maintenance beyond one grant or individual;
- handling independent forks and official-project identity.

### Domain D — offline, privacy and data-role compatibility

Assess whether the organisation can preserve:

- offline operation;
- no mandatory account or telemetry;
- user-data non-custody;
- support without routine evidence submission;
- activity-specific controller/processor assessment;
- data minimisation, retention and deletion where it handles support or complaint data;
- privacy complaint and breach routes where applicable.

A cloud service operated elsewhere in the organisation is not automatically disqualifying. The question is whether it would require ECO to abandon its controlling model.

### Domain E — security and vulnerability response

Assess evidence of:

- a monitored private security route;
- vulnerability disclosure or security policy;
- triage and incident-response capability;
- supported-version and advisory processes;
- release withdrawal or urgent warning capability;
- confidential report handling;
- access to specialist security expertise.

### Domain F — accessibility capability

Assess evidence of:

- an accessibility policy or programme;
- accessible communication routes;
- handling accessibility barriers and adjustments;
- specialist testing capability;
- representative disabled-user involvement;
- truthful conformance reporting;
- resources to remediate barriers rather than only audit them.

### Domain G — complaints, support and harmful-output response

Assess whether the organisation can distinguish and operate:

- general support;
- privacy complaints;
- accessibility complaints;
- security reports;
- misleading or harmful output incidents;
- evidence-provenance or data-loss incidents;
- urgent release warnings and withdrawal.

Published contact details alone are not proof of an operational process.

### Domain H — release, signing and supply-chain governance

Assess evidence of:

- release authority and approval procedures;
- separation of author, reviewer and releaser where practical;
- actual-build SBOM and licence capability;
- signing and key/certificate governance;
- revocation and recovery;
- reproducible or controlled builds;
- official download and verification routes;
- update, rollback and end-of-support processes.

### Domain I — repository and organisational continuity

Assess:

- multiple authorised owners or deputies;
- account and repository recovery;
- continuity during maintainer absence;
- documented emergency powers;
- protection against one personal account becoming the sole control point;
- preservation of audit records and dissenting findings.

### Domain J — financial and operational sustainability

Assess:

- recent financial reports;
- unrestricted and restricted income where disclosed;
- reliance on time-limited grants;
- staffing and technical capacity;
- reserves or continuity indicators where legitimately public;
- ability to fund legal, privacy, security, accessibility and insurance work;
- whether accepting ECO would be proportionate to the organisation's scale.

Do not treat wealth as a substitute for mission or governance fit.

### Domain K — contracts, liability and institutional capability

Assess only where relevant:

- ability and authority to contract;
- procurement experience;
- insurance and liability decision processes;
- data-role allocation;
- service and support definition;
- termination, portability and exit arrangements;
- ability to provide accurate AI and software-supply-chain disclosures.

### Domain L — legal and regulatory capability

Assess evidence that the organisation can obtain or govern appropriate advice for actual ECO features, markets and deployments, including where relevant:

- UK intended-purpose and medical-device boundaries;
- reserved legal activity and professional claims;
- accessibility and equality duties;
- product safety, consumer and liability issues;
- EU AI Act roles and deployment classification;
- EU Cyber Resilience Act roles;
- public-sector or high-consequence decision use.

No candidate receives a PASS merely because it employs lawyers or publishes policy research.

## 8. Threshold for POTENTIAL FIT

A candidate may receive `POTENTIAL FIT` for deeper due diligence only where:

- no fatal exclusion is supported;
- legal identity and authority are PASS;
- FOSS/software stewardship is PASS or supported strongly enough for the proposed role;
- offline/privacy/no-custody compatibility is PASS;
- security response is PASS or has credible operational evidence and a clearly bounded gap;
- continuity is PASS or credibly supportable without one-person dependency;
- mission fit is PASS;
- no unresolved conflict would require ECO to abandon a mandatory boundary;
- material unknowns are listed and do not undermine the threshold conclusion.

Where security, continuity, legal identity, FOSS compatibility or offline/no-custody compatibility is HOLD, the overall result cannot exceed HOLD for full stewardship.

A candidate may still be a potential limited-purpose collaborator even when unsuitable as full publisher/steward. Record the limited role separately.

## 9. Research record requirements

For each organisation preserve:

- canonical organisation name;
- legal form, registration and jurisdiction;
- assessment date;
- researcher/workstream relationship;
- proposed role being considered;
- source table;
- fatal exclusion result;
- domain decisions and reasons;
- conflicts and dependencies;
- missing evidence and questions;
- provisional status;
- confidence and freshness limitations;
- statement that no interest, endorsement or outreach authority is implied.

Do not publish:

- private personal addresses;
- non-public direct contact details;
- credentials or identity documents;
- confidential due-diligence material;
- personal casework or evidence;
- speculative allegations.

## 10. Longlist and shortlist controls

### Longlist

A longlist may include organisations with plausible category fit. Inclusion means only that public-source research is warranted.

### Shortlist

A shortlist may contain only organisations that:

- pass the fatal exclusion screen;
- have adequate identity and mission evidence;
- have no known mandatory-boundary conflict;
- justify deeper review of operational capability.

A shortlist is not an outreach list.

### Outreach-target decision

Before contact, create a separate decision record identifying:

- why the target is justified;
- which role is being explored;
- remaining unknowns;
- approved public contact route;
- approved sender role;
- exact message/pack version;
- statement that contact is non-binding;
- stop conditions.

No contact may occur under issue #62 alone.

## 11. Quality control

A research review must check:

- legal identity and status against an authoritative register where available;
- whether evidence is current;
- whether the conclusion exceeds the source;
- whether contrary evidence was omitted;
- whether inference is labelled;
- whether missing evidence was incorrectly scored positively;
- whether any wording implies interest or endorsement;
- whether personal or confidential information was included;
- whether the provisional decision follows the method.

The same technical account may post the research and review records, but the actual workstream relationship must be disclosed. Do not claim organisational independence unless the reviewer is meaningfully separate.

## 12. Stop rules

Stop research or hold a candidate where:

- legal identity cannot be verified;
- the relevant programme or organisation may be inactive;
- current governance or financial evidence is unavailable;
- sources materially conflict;
- allegations cannot be responsibly verified;
- research would require private or intrusive personal-data collection;
- the proposed role would require changing ECO's mandatory boundaries;
- the research begins to resemble negotiation or outreach without approval.

Stop before outreach where:

- the candidate is not reviewed;
- the proposed role is undefined;
- the public contact route is not approved;
- the message implies appointment, endorsement or release readiness;
- any real evidence, pilot, contract, repository transfer or signing authority is requested;
- issue #17's staged process would be bypassed.

## 13. Method acceptance criteria

Before named research begins:

- this method is merged into canonical `main`;
- the candidate register template is merged;
- mandatory boundaries and fatal exclusions match current governance;
- evidence hierarchy, freshness and uncertainty rules are usable;
- no outreach or relationship is authorised;
- exact changed-file scope and correctly classified CI evidence are preserved.

Before issue #62 can close:

- the approved method has been applied consistently;
- a public-source longlist and reviewed shortlist exist;
- fatal exclusions and missing evidence are recorded;
- no organisation is described as interested without evidence;
- a control review determines whether any separate outreach-target issue is justified;
- issue #17 and every product/release gate remain unchanged.
