# ECO controlled organisational evidence-enquiry protocol

**Issues:** #17 and #62  
**Date:** 4 August 2026  
**Status:** controlling questions-and-response protocol; no candidate, sender or contact is approved  
**Applies to:** Stage C evidence-enquiry decisions and Stage D written enquiries  
**Release effect:** none; every product and deployment gate remains closed

## 1. Plain-English position

ECO has reached the point where some organisational questions cannot be answered through public research alone.

That does not authorise outreach.

Before any organisation is contacted, ECO must separately decide:

- which exact legal organisation may receive questions;
- which exact role is being explored;
- why public evidence justifies asking;
- who may send the message;
- which public organisational route may be used;
- what may be asked;
- how any reply and personal information will be minimised, handled and deleted;
- what must cause the process to stop.

This protocol prepares those controls. It designates no organisation and authorises no message.

## 4. Purpose of Stage C and Stage D

### Stage C — controlled evidence-enquiry candidate

Stage C decides only whether one exact legal organisation has enough public role fit to justify preparing a limited written enquiry.

Stage C is not:

- a shortlist;
- a recommendation;
- a ranking;
- an expression of preference;
- an invitation to become a partner;
- organisational due diligence;
- appointment;
- contact authority.

### Stage D — non-binding written evidence enquiry

Stage D permits only an exact, separately approved written message asking whether the organisation wishes to answer limited organisational-capability questions.

Stage D is not:

- negotiation;
- a meeting request;
- a pilot proposal;
- an application for funding or sponsorship;
- a request for confidential credentials or evidence;
- transfer of duties or assets;
- consent to process real case material;
- appointment or release approval.

## 5. No default sender

No person becomes ECO's sender merely because they:

- originated the project;
- contribute code or documents;
- own or administer a GitHub account;
- prepared research;
- maintain a repository;
- use ECO;
- are named in a project record.

In particular, the originating developer is not automatically:

- ECO's publisher or supplier;
- a company director, trustee or office-holder;
- a contracting party;
- a support or complaints service;
- a controller or processor for organisational correspondence or user evidence;
- a legal, security, accessibility or regulatory representative;
- an authorised spokesperson;
- the person required to send or receive an enquiry.

## 6. Permitted future sender roles

A later Stage D decision may consider one of these roles only after explicit approval.

### Option A — established organisational intermediary

Preferred where available.

An established legal organisation or qualified intermediary may send the exact approved questions under a written, limited mandate.

The mandate must state that the intermediary:

- cannot bind ECO;
- cannot appoint a publisher;
- cannot negotiate or promise a release, role, contract, funding or pilot;
- must use only public repository information;
- must request no real evidence or confidential credentials;
- must follow the approved response-handling rules;
- must stop and return any material proposal for control review.

### Option B — specifically authorised research correspondent

An individual may act only as a **research correspondent** where they freely and explicitly accept that limited task through a separate decision.

The role must state that the person:

- writes only in an individual contributor/research capacity;
- is not ECO's appointed publisher, supplier or professional representative;
- cannot bind the project or accept duties;
- cannot offer contracts, warranties, indemnities, funding, release dates or pilots;
- cannot receive real case material;
- cannot operate general support or complaints;
- cannot continue beyond the exact approved message without further approval.

No individual is assigned this role through this protocol.

## 7. Personal-account and correspondence-route control

A Stage D message must not be sent from or routed into an individual's ordinary personal email, telephone, home address or social-media account by default.

Before contact, the Stage D decision must identify a written route that is:

- publicly attributable to the approved sender role;
- limited to the evidence enquiry;
- capable of being monitored for the approved period;
- protected by suitable access controls;
- capable of preserving and deleting the minimum necessary correspondence;
- separate from user support and real-evidence intake;
- not dependent on one unrecoverable personal account.

A personal route may be considered only through explicit, informed consent and a separate data/responsibility assessment. It is not the preferred route and must not be assumed.

No phone call, video call, physical meeting or direct-message conversation is approved at Stage D.

## 8. Stage C decision record

A proposed Stage C record must contain:

| Field | Required decision |
|---|---|
| Exact legal organisation | Legal name, number and jurisdiction |
| Exact role | Full steward or one bounded specialist role |
| Public evidence | Decision-changing evidence supporting questions |
| Contrary evidence | Conflicts, gaps and stale evidence |
| Fatal exclusions | Result for the exact role |
| Purpose of enquiry | Exact organisational facts sought |
| Stop conditions | Matters that end the proposal |
| Candidate status | Must say `EVIDENCE ENQUIRY ONLY` |
| Shortlist status | Must say `NOT SHORTLISTED` |
| Contact authority | Must say `NOT YET AUTHORISED` |
| Data boundary | Public information and organisational answers only |
| Real-evidence boundary | Prohibited |
| Review | Source-bounded control review required |

A Stage C decision does not itself approve a route, sender or message.

## 9. Stage D prerequisites

Before a message may be sent, a separate Stage D record must approve all of the following:

1. Stage C candidate and exact legal identity;
2. exact role being explored;
3. current public evidence and unresolved risks;
4. exact public organisational contact route;
5. exact sender and sender role;
6. exact subject line and body;
7. exact questions;
8. exact public documents or links to be supplied;
9. statement that no real evidence or confidential documents are requested;
10. reply-handling operator;
11. access permissions;
12. minimisation fields;
13. retention and deletion deadline;
14. public-summary/redaction rule;
15. stop and escalation conditions;
16. prohibition on unapproved follow-up;
17. control review and final authorisation.

Silence on any item means contact remains prohibited.

## 10. Permitted first-enquiry questions

A future Stage D message may ask only approved versions of questions such as:

1. **Legal entity and authority** — Which exact legal organisation, if any, would have authority to consider an ECO stewardship role?
2. **Mission fit** — Does the public-interest purpose appear potentially within that organisation's mission and powers?
3. **Boundary fit** — Could it in principle preserve free/open-source licensing, fully offline normal operation, no mandatory account/cloud/telemetry/advertising and no routine access to user evidence?
4. **Role appetite** — Would it consider receiving a formal due-diligence pack for the full publisher role or only a bounded specialist role?
5. **Security** — Does it operate or fund a confidential vulnerability route, incident response, advisories and release withdrawal suitable for an anonymously distributed desktop product?
6. **Release and signing** — Could it govern Windows release approval, Authenticode certificates, key recovery, exact source/build/SBOM/licence/hash/signature reconciliation and end of support?
7. **Support and complaints** — Could it operate accessible written routes for product support, privacy, accessibility, harmful output and security complaints without requiring real case files?
8. **Resources and continuity** — Would governing-body approval, funded staff, deputies and recovery arrangements be required before considering the role?
9. **Liability and regulation** — Could it obtain appropriate legal advice, insurance and UK/EU role assessments if the project ever approached release or supply?
10. **Conflicts and conditions** — Are there known conditions that would require cloud processing, data custody, proprietary control, an institutional pilot or another change incompatible with ECO?
11. **Next step** — Is the organisation willing to consider a later formal due-diligence stage, or should the project record that the role is outside its scope?

The first enquiry must not demand proof, documents or confidential detail. It asks whether the organisation wishes to consider providing evidence later through an approved Stage E process.

## 11. Information that must not be requested

The first enquiry must not request:

- identity documents;
- personal addresses or telephone numbers;
- credentials or access tokens;
- signing keys or certificate secrets;
- security architecture details whose disclosure would create risk;
- insurance policies or contracts as attachments;
- privileged legal advice;
- employee or trustee personal records;
- bank records;
- confidential board papers;
- real user evidence;
- health, legal, benefits, housing or complaint case files;
- private diagnostics;
- a real-user or institutional pilot;
- source, repository, domain, trademark or signing transfer.

Formal evidence may be considered only at Stage E under an approved organisational process.

## 12. Public-safe draft message structure

No message is approved by this protocol. A later Stage D decision may adapt this structure.

### Subject

`Non-binding organisational evidence enquiry about an offline open-source project`

### Opening

State:

- the sender's exact limited role;
- that ECO is an early public source-development project;
- that no publisher, product release or real-evidence use is approved;
- that the organisation has not been selected, shortlisted or described as interested;
- that the message asks only whether it wishes to answer limited capability questions.

### Project boundaries

Summarise only:

- fully offline normal operation;
- free/open-source project and approved bundled components;
- no required account, cloud, telemetry, advertising or routine evidence custody;
- one self-contained Windows application objective;
- user-controlled, source-linked evidence assistance;
- no diagnosis, professional representation, forensic guarantee, scoring or official adverse decision-making.

### Questions

Include only the exact approved questions from section 10.

### Closing

State:

- a short written decline is sufficient;
- no meeting is requested;
- no confidential material or real evidence should be sent;
- a response does not create a partnership, appointment or obligation;
- any later due diligence requires separate approval;
- the sender cannot bind ECO or accept organisational duties.

## 13. Reply minimisation

A future reply record must be limited to what is necessary to decide whether formal due diligence should be considered.

Permitted minimum fields may include:

- exact organisation;
- public route used;
- date sent and received;
- approved sender role;
- response category;
- organisation-level answers;
- stated conflicts or conditions;
- whether further written due diligence is invited;
- next control decision.

A business contact's name, title and organisational email may be retained only where necessary to understand or respond to the correspondence. They must not be published merely because they appeared in a signature.

## 14. Reply-handling and privacy controls

Before Stage D, the project must decide who operates the correspondence route and what legal/data role follows from the actual arrangement. This protocol does not predetermine controller, processor or other status.

The approved operator must:

- collect no more than the approved enquiry requires;
- keep correspondence separate from user evidence and support;
- restrict access to approved persons;
- use no response for marketing or unrelated profiling;
- publish no personal contact detail;
- create only a redacted organisation-level public summary where approved;
- apply the approved retention and deletion deadline;
- preserve an audit record of deletion or justified continued retention;
- stop if the response contains unexpected sensitive or confidential material.

No reply-handling operator or retention period exists at present.

## 15. Unsolicited attachments or confidential material

The message must tell the recipient not to send confidential material.

If an unsolicited attachment or sensitive response is received:

1. do not publish, forward widely or use it to make public claims;
2. do not open an attachment where safe handling is uncertain;
3. record only the minimum necessary fact of receipt;
4. restrict access;
5. stop the enquiry;
6. obtain a separate handling/deletion decision;
7. notify the sender where appropriate that confidential evidence was not requested;
8. do not move into due diligence automatically.

## 16. Response categories

Use only factual categories:

- `NO RESPONSE`;
- `DECLINED`;
- `OUTSIDE SCOPE`;
- `REQUESTED PUBLIC CLARIFICATION`;
- `WILLING TO ANSWER LIMITED QUESTIONS`;
- `WILLING TO CONSIDER FORMAL DUE DILIGENCE`;
- `REFERRED TO ANOTHER PUBLIC ORGANISATIONAL ROUTE`;
- `CONFLICT WITH ECO BOUNDARIES`;
- `STOPPED — UNAPPROVED REQUEST OR MATERIAL`.

Do not record `partner`, `publisher`, `selected`, `approved`, `accepted` or equivalent unless the later governing-body acceptance process is actually complete.

## 17. Stop conditions

Stop and return to issue #17/#62 control review where a recipient requests or proposes:

- real case material or identifiable user data;
- a real-user, institutional, government, healthcare or justice pilot;
- a phone call or meeting not separately approved;
- proprietary relicensing or a closed official fork;
- mandatory cloud evidence upload, accounts, telemetry or advertising;
- routine access to users' local evidence;
- unsupported legal, medical, forensic, security, accessibility or compliance claims;
- personal warranties, indemnities, contracts, support or liability from an individual contributor;
- transfer of repository, assets, domain, trademark or signing authority;
- confidential credentials or security secrets;
- public announcement of a relationship;
- formal commitment, funding or timetable;
- authority-side scoring or adverse decision use.

## 18. Follow-up boundary

One reply does not authorise a continuing conversation.

A response may be acknowledged only where the Stage D decision approved an acknowledgement. Any substantive follow-up, meeting, document exchange or due-diligence step requires a new control decision.

No automated reminders, repeated messages or pressure may be used.

## 19. Public record and audit requirements

A public-safe audit record must preserve:

- Stage C and Stage D decision identities;
- exact legal organisation and role;
- exact public route;
- sender role without private contact details;
- exact approved message hash or immutable copy;
- date/time sent;
- response category;
- redacted organisation-level outcome;
- whether the process stopped;
- retention/deletion decision;
- confirmation that no real evidence or confidential material was requested;
- confirmation that no appointment or gate change occurred.

Private correspondence must not be copied into the public repository merely to prove that it exists.
