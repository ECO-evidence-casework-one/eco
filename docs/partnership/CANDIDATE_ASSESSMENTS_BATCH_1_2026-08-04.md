## G. Conflicts and dependencies

- ECO would become organisationally part of Conservancy under a negotiated fiscal sponsorship agreement; the exact allocation of authority, assets, brand, project leadership and termination rights must be reviewed.
- Conservancy states that it generally does not control technical or artistic decisions. That protects project autonomy but means full ECO publisher, support and product-risk duties cannot be assumed.
- Conservancy's application material states that it has few staff relative to demand and may take considerable time to process new applications.
- Conservancy's mission is specifically software freedom. ECO's local-model, training-data and model-licence choices would require exact review.
- A percentage of project revenue supports Conservancy's general fund. Financial terms must be reviewed, but this is not treated as a fatal exclusion.
- Conservancy is US-based. UK/EU publisher, consumer, accessibility, data-protection, AI and product-safety roles would need separate allocation and advice.

## G. Conflicts and dependencies

- AbilityNet provides paid commercial services; ECO would need funding and a controlled scope.
- Corporate supporters and historic relationships include large technology companies. This is not treated as a conflict by itself, but independence, procurement and any technology-specific influence must be assessed.
- AbilityNet's normal services may use online forms, communication platforms and user-research processes. An ECO engagement must define no real evidence, minimal participant data and approved retention.
- AbilityNet's public material discusses compliance and legal requirements. ECO must not describe an assessment as certification, regulatory approval or a guarantee of accessibility.
- Native Windows desktop testing, Narrator/NVDA, keyboard-only use, high DPI, cognitive accessibility, long documents, low-spec hardware and offline AI error states must be explicit deliverables rather than assumed from general WCAG expertise.
- Accessibility advice must not be allowed to alter ECO's medical/legal/professional-output boundary or suggest unsafe emergency functionality.

## H. Material unknowns and due-diligence questions

1. Can AbilityNet test a native offline Windows application on the exact inexpensive hardware baseline?
2. Can the work use only synthetic matters and documents with no real case material?
3. Which assistive technologies, DPI levels, keyboard routes and cognitive-accessibility methods would be covered?
4. Can disabled-user research be conducted without ECO receiving participant personal data?
5. What reports, raw evidence, recordings and personal data would be created, who controls them and how long are they retained?
6. Can the findings and limitations be published openly under terms compatible with ECO's FOSS governance?
7. What insurance applies to the exact service and what exclusions/caps would apply?
8. How would conflicts arising from corporate supporters or other clients be handled?
9. Can AbilityNet review AI interaction states without presenting itself as verifying factual, medical or legal correctness?
10. What complaints or remediation route applies if the assessment itself is inaccessible or incomplete?

## 4. Batch decision

The research supports a **multi-organisation capability model** more strongly than a single-organisation assumption:

- an accountable legal publisher/steward would still need to own the complete issue #17 duties;
- a FOSS fiscal/legal host may solve only organisational, asset, licence and contracting parts;
- a civic-tech collaborator may contribute product and public-interest software experience without becoming publisher;
- an accessibility partner may provide specialist assurance without controlling the repository or product.

This is an analytical observation, not a decision to split the future role or contact multiple organisations.
