# Evidence & Casework One — public-safe partner information pack

**Status:** exploratory information for established organisations  
**Project stage:** early public source development  
**Partnership status:** no publisher, steward, partner or institutional deployment is appointed or agreed  
**Information boundary:** public repository information and synthetic examples only

## 1. Purpose of this pack

This pack explains Evidence & Casework One (ECO) to established organisations that may wish to explore supporting or stewarding a public-interest, free and open-source software project.

It is intended to support a **non-binding written discussion**. Reading, sharing or discussing this pack does not:

- appoint the recipient as ECO's publisher, steward or partner;
- create a contract, commitment, endorsement or exclusivity arrangement;
- transfer repository, signing, support or legal duties;
- approve a public release, real-evidence use or institutional deployment;
- require the originating developer or another contributor to form an organisation or accept an office or liability role.

## 2. What ECO is

ECO is an offline Windows desktop application under development to help a user preserve, organise, search, review and understand evidence and casework held on their own computer.

The intended product is a calm, plain-language workspace that keeps the user connected to original source material while distinguishing:

1. original source text;
2. machine extraction or OCR;
3. an ECO-generated suggestion;
4. a deliberate user confirmation;
5. a user-authored note.

Potential user-controlled functions include:

- importing and preserving files;
- hashing and integrity checks;
- document viewing and navigation;
- extraction and OCR;
- exact search and source passage retrieval;
- locating document-stated names, dates, events, deadlines and requested actions;
- source-linked summaries, chronologies and comparisons;
- helping a user prepare questions, notes, letters and draft responses;
- identifying uncertainty, possible omissions and possible inconsistencies for the user to examine.

These are intended functions and development objectives. They are not a claim that the current application is complete, reliable or approved for real evidence.

## 3. The public-interest problem

People may need to understand large collections of documents when dealing with matters such as:

- benefits and public services;
- housing and homelessness;
- healthcare and medical evidence;
- complaints and safeguarding;
- employment and education;
- consumer, financial or contractual disputes;
- legal correspondence and proceedings.

Existing workflows can require users to move between folders, email, office files, PDFs, screenshots, notes, timelines and correspondence. The material may be difficult to search, compare and explain, particularly for people affected by disability, distress, trauma, low digital confidence or limited access to professional help.

ECO's intended public-interest contribution is a private local tool that helps a user work with their own material while preserving source visibility and avoiding routine cloud transfer of their case files.

ECO does not claim that software can replace professional advice, institutional responsibility or human judgement.

## 4. Controlling product boundaries

The intended official product must preserve the following boundaries.

### Fully offline application operation

- No required cloud service.
- No required internet connection for normal application operation.
- No cloud AI fallback.
- No hidden online service.

### No user-account or surveillance model

- No required ECO account.
- No advertising.
- No routine telemetry or analytics.
- No mandatory registration merely to create a user-contact list.

### User-data non-custody

- The project is not intended to receive, host or administer users' case files.
- Ordinary support must not require users to submit real evidence.
- Public issues and tests must use synthetic, non-sensitive material.

### Free and open source

- ECO project code and approved bundled components must remain free and open source.
- Exact dependency, model and runtime licences must be reconciled before distribution.
- Corresponding source, notices and actual-build provenance are release requirements.

### Intended Windows delivery

- The intended official delivery model is one self-contained Windows desktop application.
- Users should not be required to install or manage a browser server, Python, a model, a runtime, DLL folders or separate application assets.
- The controlling hardware objective is an inexpensive Windows 11 laptop with 8 GB RAM.

These are product requirements. The exact final executable has not yet proved all of them.

## 5. Intended-purpose and safety boundary

ECO may assist a user with supplied health, legal or other sensitive evidence. Document subject matter alone is not a reason for a blanket refusal.

ECO must remain a **user-controlled evidence assistant**, not a professional or official decision-maker.

ECO must not be presented, configured or relied upon to:

- diagnose an illness or infer an unstated diagnosis;
- recommend medicine, treatment or a clinical course;
- perform prognosis, triage, monitoring or clinical-risk assessment;
- act as an emergency or safeguarding service;
- conduct litigation or provide authorised legal representation;
- guarantee legal correctness, admissibility, authenticity or outcome;
- act as a forensic laboratory or expert witness;
- score eligibility, entitlement, credibility, honesty or dangerousness;
- grant, deny, reduce, revoke or reclaim a benefit, service, right or opportunity;
- make or materially influence an official adverse decision;
- claim certification, regulatory approval or professional authority without exact evidence and approval.

A source-linked summary of what a supplied document says is different from a diagnosis, legal conclusion or eligibility decision. ECO's design and tests must preserve that difference.

## 6. Current project position

ECO remains an early source-development project.

Current restrictions include:

- no approved signed end-user release;
- no use with real, sensitive or irreplaceable evidence;
- no approved public executable download;
- no appointed publisher or operating organisation;
- no approved ordinary-user testing;
- no approved institutional, healthcare, justice-sector or EU deployment;
- no claim of complete production OCR or reliable generative local AI;
- no claim of legal, medical, forensic, security, accuracy, accessibility or regulatory compliance.

The public repository contains source, governance, release controls, limitations and audit records. Later source commits and successful CI runs do not automatically create a release.

## 7. Why ECO is exploring organisational stewardship

A sustainable official release requires more than source code.

An accountable organisation would eventually need to operate or govern matters such as:

- release approval and withdrawal;
- exact binary, source, SBOM, licence, manifest, hash and signature reconciliation;
- private vulnerability reporting and security advisories;
- supported-version and end-of-support decisions;
- privacy, accessibility, product-complaint and support routes;
- signing, revocation and recovery;
- repository and organisational continuity;
- official project identity and false-endorsement controls;
- institutional contracts, liability, insurance and exit arrangements;
- legal and regulatory assessments for actual markets and deployments.

Those are organisational responsibilities. They must not depend solely on an individual contributor's spare time, personal account or personal legal capacity.

## 8. Preferred organisational route

ECO's current preferred route is an **existing established organisation** with compatible public-interest and open-source values, such as:

- a public-interest technology organisation;
- an open-source foundation or operational steward;
- a digital-rights or access-to-justice organisation;
- an accessibility or disability organisation with sustainable technology capacity;
- an established nonprofit working with evidence, casework or public services;
- a university or research institution with sustainable software operations;
- another established legal organisation able to preserve ECO's controlling boundaries.

This list describes categories only. It does not state that any named organisation is interested, suitable or approved.

ECO does not presently require the originating developer or another contributor to create a company, community interest company, charity or other entity or to become a director, trustee or equivalent office-holder.

A new-entity proposal would require a separate future assessment of office-holders, duties, governance, funding, reporting and the reason an established host cannot perform the role.

## 9. What ECO would seek from an exploratory organisation

An initial discussion would examine whether the organisation could, in principle:

- preserve the fully offline, free/open-source and user-data non-custody model;
- understand ECO's intended-purpose and prohibited-use boundaries;
- provide sustainable governance rather than informal personal responsibility;
- support independent technical, legal, privacy, accessibility and security review;
- operate private vulnerability, complaint and support routes;
- govern release signing, withdrawal and continuity;
- maintain transparent public project records;
- undertake due diligence before accepting any formal role.

The first conversation should identify mission fit and major incompatibilities. It should not ask the organisation to accept duties immediately.

## 10. What ECO is not seeking

ECO is not seeking:

- proprietary relicensing or a closed official fork;
- cloud processing of users' evidence as the normal product model;
- mandatory accounts, telemetry, analytics or advertising;
- routine project access to users' case files;
- personal warranties, indemnities, support duties or liability from an individual contributor;
- premature claims that ECO is certified, compliant, secure, accurate, accessible, court-ready, forensic, production-ready or release-ready;
- a real-evidence or institutional pilot while current gates remain open;
- a partner announcement based only on informal interest;
- an organisation willing to lend its name without operating the required duties.

## 11. Engagement stages

### Stage 1 — public-safe exploration

Permitted:

- exchange public repository information;
- use synthetic examples;
- discuss mission fit, project boundaries and organisational capability;
- record non-binding interest and questions.

Not permitted:

- announcing a partner or publisher;
- transferring repository or signing authority;
- sharing real evidence or private diagnostics;
- agreeing institutional supply or a real-data pilot;
- implying endorsement or release readiness.

### Stage 2 — due diligence

A candidate organisation would complete ECO's publisher acceptance checklist and provide evidence concerning:

- legal identity and governing authority;
- open-source and product-boundary commitment;
- security and vulnerability response;
- support, privacy, accessibility and complaint handling;
- signing, repository and continuity controls;
- funding and operational capacity;
- contracting, liability, insurance and exit capability where relevant.

Confidential due-diligence evidence must use an approved private process and must not be placed in the public repository.

### Stage 3 — governing-body acceptance

The candidate organisation's authorised governing body would need to approve a written record stating:

- duties accepted and excluded;
- authorised role-holders and deputies;
- funding and resources;
- conditions and dependencies;
- effective and review dates;
- withdrawal or termination process.

An informal email, staff discussion or technical collaboration is not organisational acceptance.

### Stage 4 — operational validation

Synthetic or tabletop exercises would need to test matters such as:

- private vulnerability reporting;
- complaint handling;
- release withdrawal;
- significant-incident communication;
- repository/account recovery;
- signing revocation and recovery;
- primary-maintainer unavailability.

### Stage 5 — separate release decision

Organisational acceptance would not itself approve ECO.

The exact release candidate would still need to pass all application, evidence-integrity, security, accessibility, provenance, licensing, signing and distribution gates.

## 12. What ECO can offer an exploratory organisation now

At the current stage, ECO can provide:

- a public source repository;
- current project-status and release-gate records;
- intended-purpose and claims governance;
- publisher/stewardship governance and a due-diligence checklist;
- known limitations and roadmap records;
- synthetic test and audit evidence where publicly preserved;
- a written list of open blockers and required next actions;
- non-binding written discussion about mission fit and governance needs.

ECO cannot presently offer:

- an approved public product;
- real-user or real-evidence pilots;
- proven production support obligations;
- guaranteed funding, timetable or release date;
- a completed regulatory classification for every future deployment;
- contracts, warranties or institutional commitments.

## 14. Suggested first-discussion questions

A non-binding first discussion may ask:

1. Does ECO's public-interest purpose align with the organisation's mission?
2. Can the organisation preserve the offline, free/open-source and no-user-data-custody model?
3. Does it have sustainable software governance, security, accessibility and support capacity?
4. Which responsibilities could it explore, and which are outside its scope?
5. What evidence would its governing body require before considering a formal role?
6. Would it require a different product, data or commercial model that conflicts with ECO's controlling boundaries?
7. What independent expertise or funding would be needed for further due diligence?
8. Who would have authority to approve or reject a future organisational role?
