## 1. Control statement

This record tests whether the Open Knowledge Foundation (OKFN) has enough public evidence to justify consideration for the complete or near-complete organisational role defined by ECO issue #17 and the publisher/stewardship gate.

This is deliberately different from the first assessment batch. The first batch examined limited specialist roles. This record examines whether one organisation might plausibly accept and operate most or all of ECO's official publisher responsibilities.

`HOLD` means that the organisation has unusually relevant public evidence, but material full-role evidence is missing, stale, ambiguous or potentially conflicting. It does not mean unsuitable, interested, available, endorsed, shortlisted or approved for contact.

## 3. Important legal-status caution

OKFN's governance page says it holds an NGOsource Equivalency Determination certificate equivalent to a United States public charity, but the same page states that certificate was valid through **December 2025**.

At the 4 August 2026 assessment date, that wording is stale unless a renewed certificate exists elsewhere. This assessment therefore does not treat current US-public-charity equivalency as proven.

The sources reviewed establish an active England-and-Wales company limited by guarantee and OKFN's self-description as a nonprofit. They do not by themselves establish every tax, charity, public-benefit or regulatory status that might matter to a future ECO arrangement.

## 4. Reason for inclusion

OKFN is the first longlist organisation assessed for a full or near-full role because its public record combines several capabilities that the first-batch organisations held only separately:

- a longstanding UK legal entity and board structure;
- audited accounts and a finance/operations function;
- open-source and open-standards governance;
- development and maintenance of digital infrastructure and digital public goods;
- technical staff and project management;
- institutional consultancy and contracting;
- a current cross-platform desktop application;
- experience with offline/local-first software and local AI;
- accessibility and user-research activity;
- community governance, code-of-conduct and complaint handling;
- international, public-sector, civil-society and policy experience.

This combination makes deeper scrutiny reasonable. It does not prove that OKFN would accept ECO, fund it, preserve all ECO boundaries or operate every required publisher duty.

## 9. Positive evidence specific to ECO's technical model

OKFN's Open Data Editor materially strengthens its relevance beyond generic open-data reputation:

- it is a current open-source desktop application;
- it supports Windows, macOS and Linux distribution;
- it is described as local-first and suitable for offline or low-resource contexts;
- it has a local-model AI component designed to avoid cloud transmission;
- it targets non-technical users and uses user research and UX work;
- it maintains documentation and release communication;
- it has received Digital Public Good recognition;
- it demonstrates that OKFN can move from standards/policy into maintained end-user software.

Those points do not establish ECO conformance. ODE's AI model is downloaded separately, its subject is tabular-data quality, and the reviewed public material does not prove ECO-grade evidence preservation, casework boundaries, one-file packaging, signing or high-consequence controls.

## 10. Conflicts and dependencies

### Hosted and institutional operating model

OKFN provides cloud and on-premises data portals, data publication, policy support and institutional consultancy. ECO requires fully offline normal operation and no routine publisher custody of personal evidence. The two models can coexist only if ECO is contractually, technically and operationally ring-fenced.

### Government and high-consequence context

OKFN has substantial government, public-sector, digital-public-infrastructure and AI-policy activity. That experience could be valuable, but it creates a material boundary risk: ECO must not be converted into an authority-side eligibility, profiling, scoring or adverse-decision tool.

### Funding and programme direction

OKFN combines grants, institutional services and partnerships. Its board minutes recorded uncertainty caused by changes in US funding and discussed shifts toward EU/UN opportunities. A future ECO role must not depend on one short grant or require mission changes to satisfy funders.

### Product architecture mismatch

ODE is cross-platform and its local AI requires a model download. ECO currently requires one self-contained Windows executable with no user-managed model/runtime. OKFN's experience is relevant, but its existing architecture cannot be copied as proof that ECO's delivery requirement is feasible or accepted.

### Privacy evidence freshness

OKFN's public privacy policy dates from 2018, although it refers to current tools such as Plausible. For a product processing highly sensitive local casework, a current deployment-specific data-flow and support-intake assessment is mandatory.

### Equivalency certificate wording

The governance page's NGOsource certificate is stated as valid only through December 2025. A future assessment must obtain the current position or remove reliance on it.

### Service and warranty boundaries

OKFN's general terms say free web services may change or cease and make no quality promises absent a specific service agreement. ECO's official publisher would need explicit supported-version, security, withdrawal, continuity and complaint obligations rather than reliance on general web terms.

## 12. Provisional decision

**Status:** `HOLD` for a possible full or near-full accountable ECO publisher/steward role.

### Reasoned conclusion

OKFN has the strongest public evidence reviewed so far for a near-full role. It is an active UK legal entity with board governance, audited reporting, operations/finance, technical and project staff, institutional contracting, long-running FOSS infrastructure, current end-user desktop software, local-first/offline experience, local AI, user research and accessibility work.

However, ECO's complete publisher role is not a general mission-fit decision. Public evidence does not yet prove private vulnerability handling, supported versions, release withdrawal, Authenticode/signing governance, exact supply-chain provenance, separated complaints/support, insurance, product liability, regulatory ownership, synthetic-only pre-gate work or strict separation from hosted/public-authority uses.

The privacy policy and NGOsource certificate wording also require freshness correction. These gaps are too central to score as `POTENTIAL FIT` for the full role.
