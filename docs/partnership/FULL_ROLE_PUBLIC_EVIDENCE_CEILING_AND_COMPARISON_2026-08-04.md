## 1. Plain-English conclusion

Public-source research has now reached its useful limit for the two full-role organisations assessed.

The project can verify legal identity, public governance, mission, published policies, public software, operational claims, filings, repositories and visible incident or support practices.

The project cannot prove through public websites alone that an organisation:

- wants ECO;
- has governing-body authority and appetite to accept it;
- will preserve ECO's exact offline, FOSS and no-custody requirements;
- has approved staff, budget, insurance and legal advice;
- will become the official publisher;
- will operate confidential vulnerability, complaints, signing and withdrawal controls;
- will accept UK/EU regulatory ownership;
- can pass ECO-specific tabletop tests.

Repeating public searches cannot turn those unknowns into evidence.

The existing process also contains a procedural deadlock: it says a full-role candidate cannot enter a shortlist while private or organisational evidence is missing, but that evidence cannot normally be obtained without a controlled enquiry to the organisation.

The correction is to distinguish:

1. **public-source assessment**;
2. **controlled evidence-enquiry candidacy**;
3. **formal due diligence**;
4. **qualified shortlist**;
5. **governing-body acceptance**.

No organisation is advanced to any new stage through this document.

## 2. Why this record is necessary

ECO needs one accountable official publisher/steward. The current governance correctly prevents:

- reputation-based selection;
- implied partnerships;
- personal duties falling onto an individual contributor;
- informal responsibility split among specialists;
- outreach without exact target, role, route and message approval.

However, an organisation cannot publicly publish an ECO-specific board resolution, insurance decision, signing plan or acceptance of duties before it knows ECO exists and is asked whether it wishes to consider the role.

A rule requiring that evidence before any controlled enquiry would make the organisational gate impossible to complete.

The solution is not to weaken the final publisher gate. It is to add a narrow intermediate decision that authorises **questions**, not partnership, appointment or shortlist status.

## 3. Evidence vocabulary

For the comparison below:

| Decision | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `PUBLICLY SUPPORTED` | Current authoritative or first-party public evidence supports the proposition in general |
| `PARTLY SUPPORTED` | Relevant capability is visible, but ECO-specific scope or accountable role is missing |
| `NOT PUBLICLY ESTABLISHED` | Reviewed public evidence does not prove the requirement |
| `ORGANISATIONAL EVIDENCE REQUIRED` | Only the organisation or its authorised advisers can normally provide the evidence |
| `OPERATIONAL TEST REQUIRED` | A synthetic exercise or observed process is necessary |
| `CONFLICT REQUIRES RESOLUTION` | Public operating practice differs materially from ECO and must be expressly ring-fenced or changed |
| `FATAL CONFLICT` | Reliable evidence establishes an incompatible mandatory condition |

Missing evidence is not scored positively and is not proof that capability is absent.

## 6. Public-evidence ceiling

Further public searching should occur only where it can change a specific decision, such as:

- a new corporate filing;
- a newly published security policy;
- a new desktop signing or SBOM process;
- a new support, complaint or accessibility policy;
- a materially different organisational structure;
- a new public commitment directly relevant to ECO boundaries.

Do not repeatedly search the same pages hoping that absence will become proof.

The following cannot be completed by more public searching alone:

- ECO-specific board acceptance;
- commitment to ECO's boundaries;
- role holders and deputies;
- approved resources and budget;
- private procedures and evidence;
- insurance and legal advice;
- contracts and liability allocation;
- operational tests.

## 7. Corrected decision stages

### Stage A — public longlist

Purpose: identify organisations worth public-source research.

Effect: none. No suitability or interest implied.

### Stage B — public-source role assessment

Purpose: verify identity, mission, public capability, fatal conflicts, contrary evidence and unknowns.

Possible outcomes:

- `POTENTIAL FIT` for a limited role;
- `HOLD`;
- `UNSUITABLE`;
- `NOT ASSESSED`.

Effect: no contact or shortlist.

### Stage C — controlled evidence-enquiry candidate

Purpose: decide whether public evidence is strong enough to justify asking one organisation whether it is willing to provide missing organisational evidence.

This is **not** a shortlist, partnership proposal or appointment.

A Stage C decision must separately record:

- exact legal organisation;
- exact role being explored;
- why public evidence justifies an enquiry;
- unresolved risks and fatal stop conditions;
- public organisational contact route;
- approved sender role;
- exact written questions and message;
- no-real-evidence and non-binding wording;
- response-handling and deletion rules;
- prohibition on meetings, pilots, contracts or transfers unless separately approved.

No Stage C candidate exists through this document.

### Stage D — non-binding evidence enquiry

Purpose: obtain organisational facts that cannot be verified publicly.

Permitted content may include questions about:

- governing authority and mission fit;
- willingness to preserve ECO boundaries;
- security, signing, complaints and support capability;
- resources, insurance and legal/regulatory capacity;
- whether the organisation wishes to consider formal due diligence.

The message must not:

- state or imply selection;
- request real case material;
- propose a pilot;
- transfer duties or assets;
- request confidential credentials;
- promise funding, release or partnership.

No Stage D contact is authorised through this document.

### Stage E — formal due diligence

Purpose: inspect public and confidential organisational evidence under approved handling arrangements.

This stage completes the publisher acceptance checklist domains that public research cannot satisfy.

### Stage F — qualified shortlist

An organisation may enter a qualified shortlist only after material due-diligence evidence supports:

- legal authority;
- mandatory boundary acceptance;
- security, signing, support and complaint capability;
- continuity, liability and insurance;
- regulatory ownership;
- credible resources;
- no fatal conflict.

A qualified shortlist is still not appointment or release approval.

### Stage G — governing-body acceptance and operational validation

The organisation's governing body must accept the exact role, and synthetic/tabletop tests must prove the principal controls.

Only then may issue #17 approach closure. Release remains a separate gate.

## 9. Why this does not weaken safeguards

The corrected stages strengthen the project because they:

- prevent private unknowns being guessed from reputation;
- prevent “shortlist” language before due diligence;
- require exact legal identity before any question is sent;
- require a separate approved message and sender;
- keep contact public-safe, written and non-binding;
- prohibit real evidence and pilots;
- preserve every final publisher acceptance test;
- prevent individual contributors acquiring duties by implication;
- allow evidence gaps to be resolved without pretending they are already satisfied.
